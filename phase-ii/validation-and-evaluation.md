
# Phase II — AI-Assisted Development & Functional Evaluation

## Overview

Phase II incorporates AI-assisted application development within a human-directed design, testing, and refinement process.

My responsibilities include translating business requirements into functional expectations, directing implementation, evaluating generated outputs, identifying defects, and validating application behavior.

This document describes generalized development and evaluation practices rather than internal prompts, source code, testing records, or proprietary implementation details.

---

## Requirements Definition

Development activities begin with identifying a business problem and defining the expected application behavior.

Relevant considerations include:

- Intended user outcomes.
- Functional requirements.
- Expected inputs and outputs.
- Processing consistency.
- User experience.
- Exception handling.
- Data accuracy.
- Business acceptance criteria.

Clear requirements establish a basis for determining whether an implementation meets its intended purpose.

---

## AI-Assisted Implementation

Generative AI is incorporated into the iterative development process to support application functionality, technical problem-solving, and refinement.

My role includes directing implementation activities, evaluating proposed solutions, identifying incorrect assumptions, and determining whether generated functionality aligns with business requirements.

Generated outputs are subject to human evaluation rather than being accepted solely on the basis of successful generation.

---

## Functional Testing & Evaluation

Testing focuses on comparing observed application behavior against defined expectations.

General evaluation categories include:

### Functional Accuracy

Does the application perform the intended operation and produce the expected result?

### Requirements Alignment

Does the implementation satisfy the original business objective and defined constraints?

### Exception Handling

Does the application respond appropriately to incomplete, inconsistent, or unexpected information?

### Workflow Consistency

Does the functionality behave consistently across relevant operating scenarios?

### User Experience

Can intended users locate, interpret, and act upon relevant information efficiently?

### Output Validation

Are generated records, documents, and analytical outputs appropriate for their intended purpose?

---

## Iterative Refinement

Development follows a generalized evaluation cycle:

1. Define the business requirement.
2. Establish expected functionality.
3. Direct implementation.
4. Evaluate generated outputs.
5. Identify discrepancies or defects.
6. Refine the implementation.
7. Retest against functional expectations.
8. Incorporate appropriate user feedback.

This cycle supports incremental improvement and helps identify gaps between intended and observed functionality.
